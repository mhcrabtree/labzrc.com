Anti-squat and kickup

Pills, inserts, and blocks change the angle of the suspension arm pivots, which changes how the chassis behaves under power and over bumps.

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These numbers come from general tuning guidance, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time.

What anti-squat is

Anti-squat is the angle of the rear arm's pivot line relative to the ground. It decides how much the rear squats when you accelerate.

More anti-squat

  • Less squat under throttle
  • More on-power steering
  • Flatter jumps

Less anti-squat

  • More squat and rear hook-up under power
  • More rear traction
  • Less on-power steering

What kickup is

Kickup is the angle of the front arm pivot line. It changes how the front end reacts to bumps and throttle.

More kickup

  • Better over bumps
  • More on-power steering response

Less kickup

  • More stable under braking
  • Calmer off-power but digs on rough tracks

Pill and insert settings

Different cars label them differently: degrees, letters, or colored inserts. The effect is the same. Record the setting in your log and compare it to the kit manual, because the labels are not the same from car to car.

Starting points

  • High-bite clay: 1-2 degrees of anti-squat.
  • Medium/low bite: 2-3 degrees.
  • Bumpy tracks: more front kickup; smooth tracks: less.

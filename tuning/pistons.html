Pistons and hole sizes

Pistons change how the oil flows through the shock. Hole size and hole count work together with oil weight.

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These numbers come from general tuning guidance, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time.

How to think about it

Damping comes from oil weight and total hole area. A smaller total hole area behaves like thicker oil; a larger one behaves like thinner oil. The difference is that pistons change damping more at high shock speed (jumps, hard landings), while oil weight changes damping at all speeds.

PistonTotal hole areaFeel
3-hole 1.6SmallerMore damping; good for smooth high bite
3-hole 1.7MediumA balanced starting point
3-hole 1.8LargerLess damping, more compliance
2-hole 1.6 to 1.7Smaller than 3-holeCommon on 2WD fronts
4-holeLarger spreadSmoother action on bumps

Smaller holes (1.6)

Smaller / fewer

  • More damping at speed
  • Controls jumps and landings
  • Steadier on smooth tracks

Costs

  • Harsher on bumps
  • Slower to recover
  • Can reduce grip on rough sections

Larger holes (1.7, 1.8)

Larger / more

  • More compliance and grip
  • Faster shock movement
  • Better on rough tracks

Costs

  • Less control on landings
  • Chassis can feel loose
  • May need thicker oil to compensate

Front vs rear

  • Front: smaller holes steady the entry; larger holes add steering on rough tracks.
  • Rear: smaller holes keep the rear planted over jumps; larger holes add grip when the rear feels skittish.
  • Use the kit piston as your start. Change oil first, then pistons, so you know which change did what.

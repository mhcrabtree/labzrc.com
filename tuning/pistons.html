Pistons and hole sizes

The piston changes how oil flows through the shock. Hole size and hole count work together with oil weight.

Last edited: November 26, 2026 · 1/10 off-road buggies, dirt and clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These effects are general tuning principles and the numbers are starting ranges, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time, and change the front and rear separately.

Pistons and hole sizes

The piston changes how the oil flows. Smaller or fewer holes mean more damping; bigger or more holes mean less.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

Smaller or fewer holes (front)

  • Steering: Calmer, more controlled turn-in with less nose flutter.
  • Traction: Less front compliance over small bumps.
  • Stability: Better control on landings and under braking; resists nose bounce.

Bigger or more holes (front)

  • Steering: More initial steering and bite on a rough track.
  • Traction: The front follows bumps better.
  • Stability: The nose can bounce or bottom out on big landings.

Rear end

Smaller or fewer holes (rear)

  • Steering: The rear stays planted, so the car tracks straighter on power.
  • Traction: Less rear grip over bumps.
  • Stability: Strong control on jumps and exit; resists squat bounce.

Bigger or more holes (rear)

  • Steering: More rotation if the rear is too free.
  • Traction: More rear grip over bumps and on loose dirt.
  • Stability: Can bounce or bottom out on landings.

Pistons change damping most at high shock speed (landings and hard bumps); oil weight changes it at every speed. Change the oil first, then the pistons.

Piston sizes

A smaller total hole area behaves like thicker oil; a larger one behaves like thinner oil. Brands number pistons differently, so treat the table as a guide to the trend.

PistonTotal hole areaFeel
3-hole 1.6SmallerMore damping; good for smooth high bite
3-hole 1.7MediumA balanced starting point
3-hole 1.8LargerLess damping, more compliance
2-hole 1.6 to 1.7Smaller than 3-holeCommon on 2WD fronts
4-holeLarger spreadSmoother action on bumps

How to change them

  • Start on the kit pistons. Change the oil first, then pistons, so you know which change did what.
  • Make the same kind of change at both ends only when the whole car feels too stiff or too soft. Otherwise change one end.

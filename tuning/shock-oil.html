Shock oil

Oil controls how fast the shock moves. Thicker oil moves slowly; thinner oil moves quickly. Weight numbers (wt) are not a standard, so compare by centistokes (cSt).

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These numbers come from general tuning guidance, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time.

Treat the Kyosho matches as approximate. One chart I found lists Kyosho #300 at about 302 cSt and #400 at about 411 cSt, while Associated labels 30wt as 350 cSt and 35wt as 425 cSt. Other charts disagree. Measure against the bottle label when it matters, and keep each car on one brand of oil so your numbers stay comparable.

Thicker oil

Thicker

  • Slower chassis movement
  • Better on smooth, high-bite clay and big jumps
  • More consistent as the shocks warm up

Costs

  • Less grip over small bumps
  • Can feel stiff or skippy
  • Slower weight transfer

Thinner oil

Thinner

  • Faster chassis movement
  • Finds grip on rough, low-bite tracks
  • Better small-bump compliance

Costs

  • Can feel loose or bouncy
  • Bottoms out on big jumps
  • Oil gets thinner as it heats up

Front vs rear

  • Thicker front oil: slower turn-in, more stable entry.
  • Thicker rear oil: slower weight transfer onto the rear, more stability on power.
  • On smooth clay, many drivers run slightly thicker oil in the front than in the rear.

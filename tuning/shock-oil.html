Shock oil

Oil weight sets how fast each shock moves. Thicker oil moves slowly; thinner oil moves quickly.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

Thicker front oil

  • Steering: Slower, smoother turn-in with less initial bite.
  • Traction: Less compliance over small bumps; can feel skippy on a rough track.
  • Stability: Calmer under braking and on landings; slower weight transfer.

Thinner front oil

  • Steering: Quicker initial steering and more bite when the track is rough.
  • Traction: The front finds grip over small bumps.
  • Stability: The nose dives and rolls faster and can bounce or bottom out.

Rear end

Thicker rear oil

  • Steering: Slower weight transfer onto the rear; the car can push on exit.
  • Traction: Less rear grip over bumps.
  • Stability: Resists squat and bounce on landings; steady on power.

Thinner rear oil

  • Steering: Quicker rear response; more rotation if it is too free.
  • Traction: More rear grip off bumps and out of corners; the car hooks up.
  • Stability: Squats and bounces more and bottoms out on big jumps.

On smooth, high-bite clay many drivers run slightly thicker oil at the front than at the rear. Change both ends by one step at most, and keep each car on one brand of oil so the numbers stay comparable.

Weight vs centistokes

Weight numbers (wt) are not standardized, so compare by centistokes (cSt) when you can. Kyosho names its shock oils by number, and the retailer listings give the same oils in cSt: #350 (350 cSt) and #400 (400 cSt).

Shock position

Where the shock mounts on the tower (top) and on the arm (bottom) changes the effective spring rate and the amount of roll, front and rear.

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These numbers come from general tuning guidance, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time.

Shock tops (tower)

Tops closer together (more lean-in)

  • Softer effective rate
  • More roll
  • More grip on bumpy or low-bite tracks

Tops further apart (more upright)

  • Firmer effective rate
  • Less roll, quicker response
  • Better on smooth, high-bite clay

Shock bottoms (arm)

Bottoms outward

  • Firmer effective rate
  • Quicker response
  • Less roll

Bottoms inward

  • Softer effective rate
  • More grip over bumps
  • More roll

Using top and bottom together

Treat the two as one adjustment. Moving the top inward and the bottom inward both soften the car; moving the top outward and the bottom outward both firm it up. Moving one in and the other out cancels part of the change.

Front vs rear

  • Front: a firmer front gives quicker turn-in; a softer front gives more entry grip.
  • Rear: a firmer rear gives more response and less rear traction; a softer rear adds traction on exit.

What you are looking for

You want the car to roll just enough to find grip without feeling lazy. On smooth, high-bite clay, lean firm and upright. If the car pushes or skips on bumps, soften the position before you change springs.

Sway bars

A sway bar resists body roll. It changes the balance of the car in corners without changing how it lands.

Last edited: November 26, 2026 · 1/10 off-road buggies, dirt and clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These effects are general tuning principles and the numbers are starting ranges, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time, and change the front and rear separately.

Sway bars

A sway bar links the two wheels on an axle and resists body roll. It acts only when the wheels move differently, so it changes cornering balance without changing how the car lands.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

Thicker front bar

  • Steering: Sharper, flatter turn-in with less roll.
  • Traction: Less front grip mid-corner, especially on bumps.
  • Stability: Steadier through rhythm sections.

Thinner front bar

  • Steering: More front grip and steering mid-corner.
  • Traction: More compliance.
  • Stability: More roll; slower response.

Rear end

Thicker rear bar

  • Steering: More rotation on power; the rear gets looser.
  • Traction: Less rear traction mid-corner and on exit.
  • Stability: Flatter; helps on jumps.

Thinner rear bar

  • Steering: A calmer rear; may push.
  • Traction: More rear traction.
  • Stability: More roll; the car can feel lazy.

When to change them

  • Front pushes in corners: try a thinner front bar, or a thicker rear bar.
  • Rear is loose in corners: try a thinner rear bar, or a thicker front bar.
  • Bumpy track: go thinner at the end that skips. Smooth track: go thicker for a flatter car.

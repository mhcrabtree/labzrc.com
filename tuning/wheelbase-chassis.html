Wheelbase, chassis, and optional parts

What changes when the car gets longer, shorter, wider, or narrower, and what the ZX-7.5 option parts actually do.

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

From Kyosho's manual. Rows marked (manual) come from the Lazer ZX-7.5 instruction manual, No. 34331.

Less verified than the other pages. The manual says which options exist but not what each one does, and I found no ZX-7.5 tuning guide. The effects below are general chassis tuning principles. Test each change on the track.

Kit dimensions

A retailer listing (Big Squid RC) gives the ZX-7.5 as 278 mm wheelbase and 248 mm width. I did not find these in the manual, so measure your own car.

Longer vs shorter wheelbase

Longer wheelbase

  • More stable in a straight line and over jumps
  • Smoother over bumps and rhythm sections
  • Less steering response and slower rotation

Shorter wheelbase

  • Quicker rotation, more agile in tight corners
  • More steering response
  • Can feel nervous and easier to spin out

Which end you move matters. A longer rear (axle moved back) usually adds rear stability and traction on power; a longer front usually adds stability and takes away some turn-in. On a small, tight track like yours, shorter and more agile is usually worth trying first, but only after the base setup is working.

Wider vs narrower track

Wider at one end

  • More stability and roll resistance at that end
  • Less weight transfer, flatter feel

Narrower at one end

  • More roll at that end
  • More grip at that end on a slick track, but less stable

A wider front usually means less front grip and a calmer car; a wider rear usually means less rear grip and more rotation. Change one end at a time.

Stabilizer bars in practice

Thicker front bar

  • Flatter turn-in, quicker response
  • Less front roll

Thicker rear bar

  • More rear stability, less rear roll
  • Less rear grip on slick surfaces

A thinner bar does the reverse in each case. Bars interact with springs, so change one at a time.

Where to start on your track

  1. Run the kit configuration first: stock holders, front center diff, kit top plates.
  2. Move the battery before you touch the layout; it is cheap and reversible.
  3. Try the stiffer front top plate only if the car feels lazy on turn-in.
  4. Add carbon or stiffening parts last, one at a time, and keep notes on the lap times.

Wheelbase and chassis

What changes when the car gets longer, shorter, wider, or narrower, where the weight sits, and what stiffening parts do. This page is general; check your own car for which of these it lets you adjust.

Last edited: November 26, 2026 · 1/10 off-road buggies, dirt and clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These effects are general tuning principles and the numbers are starting ranges, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time, and change the front and rear separately.

Wheelbase

Longer overall wheelbase

  • Steering: Slower rotation and less turn-in.
  • Traction: Smoother over bumps; steadier on exit.
  • Stability: More stable in a straight line and over jumps.

Shorter overall wheelbase

  • Steering: Quicker rotation and more steering response.
  • Traction: Can feel nervous over bumps.
  • Stability: Easier to spin out.

Which end you move

MoveUsually does this
Front axle forward (longer front)More stability, less turn-in; the front pushes more.
Front axle back (shorter front)More steering; the car gets sharper and twitchier.
Rear axle back (longer rear)More rear traction and stability on power; less rotation.
Rear axle forward (shorter rear)More rotation; the rear gets looser.

Track width

A wider axle resists roll more, which usually means less grip at that end. A narrower axle rolls more and gains grip but is less stable.

ChangeFront endRear end
WiderCalmer car, less steeringMore stable rear, less rear grip, a bit more rotation
NarrowerMore steering, less stableMore rear grip, a bit less rotation

Weight balance

Moving the battery moves more weight than most small tweaks. In 4WD, the center diff and motor position also shift the balance.

Move weightFront endRear end
Toward the frontMore front grip and steering; may feel nose-heavy on jumpsLess rear grip and traction
Toward the rearLess front grip; the front can pushMore rear traction on power

Top plates, braces, and stiffening parts

Chassis flex adds or removes grip at each end. A stiffer part makes that end react faster and flatter; a more flexible one helps it find grip on rough or low-bite ground.

PartFront endRear end
Stiffer top plate or braceSharper steering; less front grip on rough groundMore rear response and rotation; less rear compliance
More flexible plate or no braceMore front grip on rough groundMore rear grip on rough ground
Carbon or aluminum armsLess flex; more direct steering feelLess flex; more direct rear response

Add stiffening parts last, one at a time, and keep notes on lap times. Some parts that look the same on the front and rear behave differently at each end.

Links and alignment

Turnbuckles, caster, camber, and toe shape how the car turns in, holds the middle of the corner, and exits.

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These numbers come from general tuning guidance, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time.

Turnbuckles / upper links

Longer upper link

  • Less camber gain as the car rolls
  • Calmer, more predictable

Shorter upper link

  • More camber gain
  • More grip when rolled, but more sensitive

Mounting the link higher or lower on the tower also changes the roll center. Change the length for camber and the mounting for roll center, and do not do both at once.

Caster

More caster

  • Straight-line stability
  • More on-power and mid-corner steering

Less caster

  • More initial turn-in
  • Less stability on the straight

Camber

More negative

  • More mid-corner side bite
  • Hotter inside edge on the tire

Less negative

  • More straight-line and braking grip
  • Flatter contact patch

Typical dirt starting points are front -1 to -2 degrees and rear -1 to -1.5 degrees.

Toe

Front toe-out

  • Sharper turn-in
  • Less straight-line stability

Front toe-in

  • Calmer car
  • Slower turn-in

Rear toe-in

  • More rear stability and exit grip
  • Slightly less rotation

Less rear toe-in

  • More rotation
  • Can snap loose

Quick diagnosis

SymptomTry
Pushes on entryFront toe-out, less caster, softer front
Pushes mid-cornerMore front camber, shorter front upper link
Pushes on exitMore caster, less rear anti-squat
Loose on entryFront toe-in, more rear toe-in
Loose on exitMore rear toe-in, softer rear, thinner center diff

Links and alignment

Caster, camber, toe, and the upper links shape how the car turns in, holds the middle of the corner, and exits.

Last edited: November 26, 2026 · 1/10 off-road buggies, dirt and clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These effects are general tuning principles and the numbers are starting ranges, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time, and change the front and rear separately.

Caster

Caster is the fore-and-aft lean of the front steering axis. It is a front-end setting only.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

More caster

  • Steering: Less initial turn-in, but more mid-corner and on-power steering.
  • Traction: More front grip in sustained corners as the outside tire leans in.
  • Stability: More stable and straighter on the straights.

Less caster

  • Steering: Quicker initial turn-in and more off-power steering.
  • Traction: Less mid-corner front bite.
  • Stability: The car wanders more on the straight.

The rear has no caster. Its closest equivalents are rear toe and anti-squat.

Camber

Camber is how far the top of the tire leans in (negative) or out. Typical dirt starting points are front -1 to -2 degrees and rear -1 to -1.5 degrees.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

More negative front camber

  • Steering: More mid-corner steering and side bite.
  • Traction: Less braking and straight-line grip; the tire loads its inside edge.
  • Stability: Can feel edgy; the inside edge wears faster.

Less negative front camber

  • Steering: Less mid-corner steering.
  • Traction: More braking and straight-line grip from a flatter contact patch.
  • Stability: Calmer and smoother.

Rear end

More negative rear camber

  • Steering: The rear holds a line better in corners.
  • Traction: Less forward bite on power, especially in 2WD.
  • Stability: More stable mid-corner; the inside edge wears faster.

Less negative rear camber

  • Steering: Looser mid-corner.
  • Traction: More forward bite and braking grip.
  • Stability: Can slide at the limit.

Toe

Toe is how far the fronts of the tires point in (toe-in) or out (toe-out). Typical starting points are front 0 to 1 degree out and rear 2 to 3 degrees in.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

More toe-out

  • Steering: Sharper turn-in.
  • Traction: Scrubs the tires on the straights, which costs speed.
  • Stability: Twitchy on the straights.

More toe-in

  • Steering: Slower turn-in.
  • Traction: Less tire scrub; smoother on the straights.
  • Stability: Calm and straight.

Rear end

More toe-in

  • Steering: Less rotation; the car tracks straight.
  • Traction: More rear grip on exit and under braking.
  • Stability: Much more stable and forgiving.

Less toe-in (toward zero)

  • Steering: More rotation and quicker turn-in.
  • Traction: Less rear grip on exit.
  • Stability: Can snap loose.

Turnbuckles and upper links

The length of the upper (camber) link sets how much camber changes as the chassis rolls. Its mounting height also moves the roll center.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

Longer front link

  • Steering: Less camber change as the car rolls: steady, predictable steering.
  • Traction: Less extra side grip in roll.
  • Stability: Calmer and more consistent.

Shorter front link

  • Steering: More camber gain in roll: more mid-corner steering.
  • Traction: More side bite in roll.
  • Stability: More sensitive; can feel edgy.

Rear end

Longer rear link

  • Steering: Rear camber changes little: calmer, and the car can push on exit.
  • Traction: Less rear side grip mid-corner.
  • Stability: More predictable over bumps.

Shorter rear link

  • Steering: More rear camber gain: the rear holds in corners.
  • Traction: More rear side grip in roll.
  • Stability: More sensitive over bumps.

Change the length for camber and the mounting height for roll center, but not both at once.

Quick diagnosis

SymptomTry at the frontTry at the rear
Pushes on entryFront toe-out, less caster, softer front springs or oilStiffer rear spring, less rear toe-in
Pushes mid-cornerMore front camber, shorter front upper link, thinner front barThicker rear bar, shorter rear link
Pushes on exitMore caster, more front kick-upLess rear anti-squat, softer rear spring
Loose on entryFront toe-in, stiffer front springMore rear toe-in, softer rear spring
Loose on exitThicker front diff oilMore rear toe-in, softer rear spring, thicker rear diff oil

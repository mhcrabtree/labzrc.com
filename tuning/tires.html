Tires

Tire choice changes grip more than any other adjustment. Compound sets how soft the rubber is; tread (or no tread) decides how it works with the surface.

Last edited: November 26, 2026 · 1/10 off-road buggies, dirt and clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These effects are general tuning principles and the numbers are starting ranges, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time, and change the front and rear separately.

Slicks vs treaded

Slick / smooth

  • Surface: Best on smooth, tacky, high-bite clay.
  • Grip: Maximum contact patch.
  • Watch for: Loose or dusty ground, where slicks slide.

Treaded

  • Surface: Bites on dry, dusty, or loose ground.
  • Grip: More forgiving when grip is low.
  • Watch for: Chunking or rolling over on high bite.

Tires

Tire choice changes grip more than any other adjustment. Slicks suit smooth, tacky clay; treaded tires help when the surface is dry, dusty, or loose.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

More front grip (softer or more tread bite)

  • Steering: More steering and quicker turn-in; can tip toward oversteer.
  • Traction: More front bite on entry and mid-corner.
  • Stability: The car rolls onto the tires, and tire heat builds faster.

Less front grip (firmer or less bite)

  • Steering: The car pushes; less turn-in.
  • Traction: Slides earlier but stays consistent over a run.
  • Stability: Smooth and predictable.

Rear end

More rear grip (softer or more tread bite)

  • Steering: More rear stability; the car can push on exit.
  • Traction: More forward bite and exit traction.
  • Stability: Calmer and harder to rotate.

Less rear grip (firmer or less bite)

  • Steering: Looser, with more rotation.
  • Traction: Less rear grip, especially on power.
  • Stability: Slides earlier.

In 2WD choose the rear tire for forward traction and the front for steering. In 4WD keep both ends close and bias from there.

Compounds

JConcepts uses compound names and colors that differ by tire line. In general, a softer compound grips more but wears and overheats faster; a firmer compound lasts and stays consistent. AMain's JConcepts Ellipse listing describes Silver as similar to Gold but softer, for indoor use, breaking in faster and suiting freshly groomed tracks. That is a retailer description, so confirm it on JConcepts' own compound chart.

If the track is...Try
Smooth, tacky claySlicks in a firmer, indoor compound
Medium biteA softer compound slick, or a low-profile pin tire
Dry or dustyTreaded tires in a soft compound
Wet or looseTreaded, medium-soft

Who makes them

  • JConcepts: the most common choice at local clubs, with the widest range of compounds and tread patterns.
  • Pro-Line: good alternatives in several treads and compounds.
  • Hot Race Tires and AKA: smaller names that are worth asking about at your club.

I do not have measured results for each tire and compound, so keep your own notes: tire, compound, track, temperature, and lap times.

Gearing

Final drive ratio (FDR) for the ZX-7.5 from the manual's gear chart, plus a calculator for combinations the chart skips.

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

From Kyosho's manual. Rows marked (manual) come from the Lazer ZX-7.5 instruction manual, No. 34331.

Starting points, not gospel. These numbers come from general tuning guidance, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time.

What the numbers mean

The ZX-7.5 uses 48 pitch (48P) gears; "pitch" is the tooth size, and the spur and pinion are counted in teeth (T). Final drive ratio is spur teeth ÷ pinion teeth × 2.5, where 2.5 is the ratio built into the car after the pinion and spur. The values on the manual's chart all match this formula.

Higher FDR (bigger spur or smaller pinion)

  • More acceleration and punch out of corners
  • Lower top speed
  • Cooler motor and ESC

Lower FDR (smaller spur or bigger pinion)

  • Higher top speed on long straights
  • Less punch; harder on the motor
  • Hotter motor and ESC

Picking a starting ratio

  • The kit motor guidance in the manual is 15.5T or 13.5T for 4WD, so a 17.5 is a milder motor than Kyosho assumes. That points toward a higher FDR (more pinion-to-spur reduction) than a hot motor would use on the same track.
  • On a small, tight track with a 17.5, I would start near the middle of the chart and adjust by one tooth based on motor temperature and the end of the main straight. I have not tested these numbers, so treat any specific ratio as a guess.
  • After a run, check motor and ESC temperature. If either is too hot to hold your hand on for a few seconds, gear lower (higher FDR); if the car runs cool and feels slow on the straight, gear up.
  • Check mesh with a piece of paper between the gears (the manual shows this on page 29).

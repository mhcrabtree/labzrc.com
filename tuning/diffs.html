Differentials

Diffs decide how power splits between the wheels. Thicker oil acts more locked; thinner oil lets the wheels turn more independently.

Last edited: November 26, 2026 · 1/10 off-road buggies, dirt and clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These effects are general tuning principles and the numbers are starting ranges, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time, and change the front and rear separately.

Ball diff vs gear diff

TypeProsCons
Ball diffSimple, adjustable by hand, no oil to leakWears, needs rebuilds, changes feel as it heats
Gear diff (oil)Consistent, tunable with oil weight, very smoothNeeds sealing; oil can leak; a bit more work to change

More racers are moving to gear diffs because the feel stays consistent through a race and tuning is a matter of swapping oil.

The center numbers depend on the car. Some 4WD buggies use a thin fluid center diff; others, such as Kyosho's, use a very thick oil (in the hundreds of thousands) that acts almost locked. Start from your manual's oil and move one step at a time. Kyosho's manuals also give a grip scale for the front and rear diffs, running from #2000 (low grip) to #10000 (high grip).

Differentials

Diff oil sets how freely the wheels on an axle turn relative to each other. Thicker oil acts more locked; thinner oil acts more open.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front (4WD)

Thicker front diff oil

  • Steering: Pulls straighter on power, so there is less on-power steering (push).
  • Traction: Both front tires drive evenly; good forward bite on loose dirt.
  • Stability: Calmer on entry and braking.

Thinner front diff oil

  • Steering: More steering and rotation on entry and exit.
  • Traction: The inside front tire can spin on power.
  • Stability: Twitchier; the front can dart.

Center (4WD)

Thicker center diff oil

  • Steering: More even drive front to rear; the car can push on power.
  • Traction: Strong, consistent forward bite on smooth, high-bite tracks.
  • Stability: Stable on power and under braking.

Thinner center diff oil

  • Steering: More on-power steering.
  • Traction: Power goes to the axle with less load; less drive on low bite.
  • Stability: Looser and less predictable on exit.

Rear end

Thicker rear diff oil

  • Steering: Less rotation; the car goes straighter and can push on exit.
  • Traction: Strong forward bite on loose or low-bite dirt.
  • Stability: Stable under braking and on the straights.

Thinner rear diff oil

  • Steering: More rotation and rear steering, which helps on tight tracks.
  • Traction: The inside rear tire can spin; less drive.
  • Stability: Looser on exit; can snap if too open.

2WD: the front wheels are not driven, so there is no front or center diff to tune; the rear diff does all the work. 4WD: change one diff at a time. Carpet needs different (thicker) numbers; those come later.

Diff oil at AMain Hobbies

Kyosho sells diff oil by number, and the listings give the same numbers in cSt: #5000, #7000, #40000, #200000, and #300000. 1UP Racing also sells pure silicone diff oil in a wide range of viscosities.

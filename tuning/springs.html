Springs

Springs set how much the chassis rolls, squats, and dives. Spring rate is measured in pounds per inch, and the color is just a code for a rate.

Last edited: November 25, 2026 · Dirt / clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These numbers come from general tuning guidance, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time.

Color is a code, not a standard

Every brand uses its own color code, and the same color can mean different rates on different shock sizes. Read the brand's chart before you compare colors across brands.

Aftermarket 13mm springs: 1UP Racing X-Gear

The ZX-7.5 uses DC13 big bore shocks, which take 13mm springs. 1UP Racing X-Gear 13mm buggy springs are sold at AMain Hobbies in a color ladder from soft to firm. From the listing names, front springs run:

Rear springs are a separate set with their own rates: Extra Soft/White (1UP10521) and Extra Hard/Blue (1UP10525) are the two I confirmed. A front-and-rear Pro Pack (1UP10530) is also listed.

Check fit and stock before you order. The AMain listings I saw did not give the rate in lb/in or the free length, and several showed as out of stock. The kit springs are 44 mm (front) and 61 mm (rear) long, so confirm the X-Gear lengths on 1UP's spec sheet. Also match the rate to the kit spring, not just the color.

Front springs

Firmer front

  • Quicker turn-in
  • Less roll, flatter in the corner
  • Less front grip over bumps

Softer front

  • More front grip at entry
  • Steadier over bumps
  • Slower response and more roll

Rear springs

Firmer rear

  • More rear response and steering
  • Less rear traction under power
  • Snappier over jumps

Softer rear

  • More rear traction on exit
  • More squat and roll
  • Can feel lazy over a rhythm section

Spring tips

  • Change both springs by a step, not four steps.
  • Springs and oil work together: a firmer spring often wants slightly thicker oil to avoid bounce.
  • Keep the same spring on both sides of an axle.

Springs

Springs set how much each end of the chassis rolls, dives, and squats. Rate is measured in pounds per inch, and the color is only a code for a rate.

Last edited: November 26, 2026 · 1/10 off-road buggies, dirt and clay, 17.5 stock class

Starting points, not gospel. These effects are general tuning principles and the numbers are starting ranges, not measured results from this garage. Change one thing at a time, and change the front and rear separately.

Color is a code, not a standard

Every brand uses its own color code, and the same color can mean different rates on different shock sizes. Compare by the rate printed on the package, not by color.

Springs

Spring rate sets how much each end of the chassis rolls, dives, and squats.

Each box shows what happens at that end of the car. Front and rear often do opposite things.

Front end

Stiffer front spring

  • Steering: Quicker, sharper turn-in on smooth tracks; less steering over bumps.
  • Traction: Less front grip on rough or low-bite ground; the tires skip instead of absorbing bumps.
  • Stability: Less dive under braking and less roll mid-corner; resists bottoming on landings.

Softer front spring

  • Steering: More steering at entry and mid-corner; forgiving on a bumpy track.
  • Traction: More front bite on low-bite or bumpy ground.
  • Stability: More dive and roll; slower to react, and can feel lazy or bottom out.

Rear end

Stiffer rear spring

  • Steering: The rear rotates sooner, so the car turns more but can snap loose.
  • Traction: Less rear grip and forward bite, especially over bumps and on loose dirt.
  • Stability: Flatter on jumps and resists squat; handles big landings well.

Softer rear spring

  • Steering: A calmer rear; the car can push on exit.
  • Traction: More rear grip and forward bite out of corners.
  • Stability: More squat and roll; the rear can bottom out on big jumps.

In 2WD the rear springs set drive traction directly, so go softer at the rear for low bite. In 4WD both ends pull, so the balance from front to rear matters more than either number alone.

Check fit and stock before you order. The AMain listings I saw did not give the rate in lb/in or the free length, and several showed as out of stock. Confirm the length and rate against your current springs before buying.

Spring tips

  • Change one spring step at a time, and change the front and the rear separately so you can tell which end did what.
  • Springs and oil work together: a firmer spring often wants slightly thicker oil to avoid bounce.
  • Keep the same spring on both sides of an axle.
